Every game of Magic keeps a second pile face up beside the library. Cards that were destroyed, countered, discarded or sacrificed go there, and they do not always stay put. A spell is cast again. A creature claws its way back. A forgotten trick returns at the worst possible moment for the opponent.

That pile is the **graveyard**, and the habit of pulling cards back out of it is called **graveyard recursion**. It is one of the oldest ideas in the game and one of the richest in story, because a return is never only a return. It says something about death, about memory, and about what the living are willing to pay.

This guide opens **[Behind the Cards](/articles/tag/behind-the-cards)**, a series on the ideas beneath Magic's mechanics, written for people who design their own cards. It covers the story recursion tells, how each color regards the dead, the shapes a return can take, and how to build a recursion card of your own in PipGlyph.

## Why the Graveyard Tells a Story

Most of Magic's zones are hidden or fleeting. Your hand is private; the stack empties every turn. The graveyard is public, ordered and lasting. Everything in it has a history both players watched unfold.

That makes it a natural home for memory. When a card comes back, both players remember what it did the first time and what it cost to remove. The return carries weight because the loss did.

Three story ideas sit beneath almost every recursion design:

- **The dead are not finished.** Ghosts, revenants, the bird that rises from its own ashes: the idea that death is a doorway rather than a wall runs through folklore everywhere, and Magic gives it rules.
- **Nothing returns unchanged.** What comes back is weaker, stranger, briefer, or bound to something. A return that leaves no mark reads as a bookkeeping trick rather than a story.
- **Someone pays.** Raising the dead costs mana, life, other cards, or the right to do it again. The price is where the drama lives.

When a recursion card feels flat, one of those three is usually missing.

## How Each Color Answers Death

The color pie gives each color its own attitude toward death, and its graveyard effects follow from that attitude. [The color pie guide](/articles/mtg-color-pie-explained) covers each philosophy in full; here is how those philosophies treat the dead.

| Color | What death means | What its recursion looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| White | A loss the community refuses to accept | Small, loyal creatures returned to the battlefield, often in groups or under a condition |
| Blue | Knowledge that can be recalled | Rarely creatures; more often a spell remembered, recast, or put back on top of the library |
| Black | A resource like any other | Creatures reanimated, cards returned to hand, and the graveyard itself spent as a cost |
| Red | One last burst of fury | Brief, hasty returns that end in exile, and spells that flare a second time |
| Green | Part of a cycle that feeds new growth | Cards returned to hand, especially lands and creatures, and strength drawn from what is buried |

Black is the home color of reanimation, and black and green together treat the graveyard as a compost heap: death feeding growth. That pairing is the heart of [the Golgari color pair](/articles/mtg-guilds-and-color-pairs-explained). A recursion card that ignores its color's attitude still works, but it reads like the wrong voice telling the right story.

## The Shapes of a Return

Published Magic has explored recursion through dozens of mechanics. Five shapes cover most of them, and each tells a slightly different story.

### The Second Casting

The spell itself returns. **Flashback** arrived in *Odyssey* in 2001, a block built around the graveyard, and lets you cast an instant or sorcery a second time from your graveyard for a separate cost, after which the card is exiled.[^1] The story is an echo: the same words spoken twice, usually louder and dearer the second time.

A spell you will cast twice has to be priced for both casts. The usual answer is a fair first cast and a more expensive second one, so the card is good on its own and better over a long game.

### The Brief Return

The creature comes back for one last act. **Unearth**, from the *Shards of Alara* block, belonged to Grixis, a shard of death and necromancy: it returns a creature from your graveyard for a single turn with haste, then exiles it. The story is the dead rising for one final charge.

Because the creature is exiled afterward, a brief return can cost less than a permanent one. Give the returning creature something to do in its one turn, or the return is wasted.

### The Return, Changed

The creature comes back, but not as it was. *Shadowmoor*'s **persist** returns a creature marked with a −1/−1 counter, and a marked creature does not return again; its later mirror, **undying**, returns one stronger, with a +1/+1 counter instead. *Amonkhet*'s **embalm** goes further: it exiles the card from your graveyard and makes a token copy of it, a white Zombie, the mummified servant of what the creature used to be.[^3]

The story here is transformation. Death leaves a mark, and the mark is what stops the creature returning forever.

### The Return at a Price

Some returns consume the graveyard itself. **Escape**, from *Theros Beyond Death* (2020), lets you cast a card from your graveyard by paying its escape cost, which includes exiling other cards from your graveyard.[^4] It suited a set whose story was a flight out of the Underworld; [the Nyx frame guide](/articles/mtg-nyx-frame-explained) follows that story.

The story is a toll: the dead buy their way out with other dead. Because the fuel runs out, a card can escape more than once without the game breaking, but each return is slower than the last.

### The Ghost

Some cards don't return at all. They leave something behind: a creature that dies and leaves a spirit, an enchantment that keeps a count of the fallen, a curse that outlives the hand that cast it. The story is haunting. The body is gone; the presence remains. *Innistrad* built a whole plane on that feeling, and [our Innistrad spotlight](/articles/innistrad-set-design-study) studies how.

## Pricing a Return

Recursion is card advantage. A card you can use twice is worth two cards, and a creature that keeps coming back can be the hardest threat in the game to answer. A few principles keep it fair and fun:

- **Price the whole life of the card.** Cost the first use as if it were the only one, then make the return cost something real: more mana, a condition, a counter, or exile afterward.
- **Prefer once to forever.** A return that exiles the card, marks it with a counter or burns fuel ends by itself. A creature that returns every turn for free needs an exceptional reason to exist.
- **Keep exile as the answer.** Recursion is checked by effects that exile cards from graveyards. A design that dodges exile as well, by returning from exile or shuffling itself back, removes the check the whole idea relies on.
- **Watch for loops.** Two recursion effects that return each other can form an endless loop. Read your design beside the most common sacrifice outlets before you sleeve it up.

[Designing balanced custom cards](/articles/designing-balanced-custom-mtg-cards) covers costing in general, and [how to playtest custom cards](/articles/how-to-playtest-custom-mtg-cards) covers finding loops before your playgroup does.

## Five Original Recursion Cards

Here is one example design for each shape. The names, costs and rules text are original; take them as starting points and bend them toward your own world.

### Second Toll of the Drowned Bell

<Mana cost="{1}{U}" /> · Instant

> Tap target creature. It doesn't untap during its controller's next untap step.
>
> Flashback <Mana cost="{3}{U}" />

*The second casting.* Blue delays rather than destroys, so the effect is a long stall. Flashback costs twice the first cast, keeping the first cast the efficient one. The flavor writes itself: a bell beneath the sea that rings once for a warning and once more for the drowned.

### Kilnborn Marauder

<Mana cost="{2}{R}" /> · Creature — Zombie Barbarian · 3/2

> Menace
>
> Whenever this creature attacks, it deals 1 damage to each opponent.
>
> Unearth <Mana cost="{2}{R}" />

*The brief return.* The attack trigger gives the creature a job during its one returned turn, and unearth's exile keeps the second life short. It is a raider who comes back for the last raid, not a guard who never leaves.

### Hollowmere Mourner

<Mana cost="{2}{B}" /> · Creature — Human Cleric · 2/2

> When this creature dies, create a 1/1 black Spirit creature token with flying. It has "When this token dies, each opponent loses 1 life."

*The ghost.* The mourner returns as the thing it mourned for. Each stage is smaller than the last, and the final one costs the opponent a little life on its way out, so the card fades rather than repeats.

### Barrowroot Matriarch

<Mana cost="{3}{B}{G}" /> · Legendary Creature — Elf Shaman · 5/4

> Trample
>
> Whenever this creature attacks, you may return target land card from your graveyard to your hand.
>
> Escape — <Mana cost="{3}{B}{G}" />, Exile five other cards from your graveyard.

*The return at a price.* A Golgari elder whose roots pull buried things back to the surface. Escape spends the graveyard as fuel, and the attack trigger refills a little of it, but never enough to make her truly endless.

### Lanternwake Psalm

<Mana cost="{2}{W}" /> · Sorcery

> Return target creature card with mana value 2 or less from your graveyard to the battlefield.
>
> Requiem <Mana cost="{1}{W}" /> *(You may cast this card from your graveyard for its requiem cost if a creature you controlled died this turn. If you do, exile it as it resolves.)*

*An invented keyword.* "Requiem" is our own mechanic, not a published one: a second casting that only opens after a loss. White rarely gets graveyard spells, so the condition keeps it in character, a vigil sung for someone who just fell. When you invent a keyword, keep the reminder text on its first appearance and template it the way [the templating cookbook](/articles/mtg-templating-cookbook) describes.

## Graveyard Recursion FAQ

### What Is Graveyard Recursion in MTG?

Graveyard recursion is any effect that brings a card back from the graveyard for another use: returning a creature to the battlefield, putting a card back into your hand, or casting a spell from the graveyard. Flashback, unearth, persist, undying, embalm and escape are all forms of it.

### Which MTG Color Is Best at Graveyard Recursion?

Black is the home of reanimation, returning creatures from the graveyard to the battlefield. Green returns cards to hand as part of a natural cycle, white brings back small creatures, red allows brief and furious returns, and blue rarely recurs creatures but can recall and recast spells.

### Is Graveyard Recursion Card Advantage?

Yes. A card you can use twice does the work of two cards, so a recursion card should be priced for both uses. A higher cost for the second use, a condition, or exiling the card afterward keeps it fair.
