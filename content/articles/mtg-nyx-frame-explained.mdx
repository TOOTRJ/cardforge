Look at a recent enchantment and the frame around the art is not flat colour. It is a night sky: a soft field of stars along the top and sides, the card's colour showing through it. Players call it the **Nyx frame**, after the place it was drawn to evoke. It started as the mark of one world's gods and, eleven years later, became what every enchantment looks like. Here is where it came from, what changed along the way, and how it differs from the ordinary frame.

## What is Nyx?

Nyx is the night sky of Theros, the plane Magic built from Greek myth — and it is more than a sky. In Wizards' own words it is a starry realm, visible at night, where the gods of Theros live. Mortals who sleep and dream are said to visit it. The gods are made of it: where a mortal would cast a shadow or show bare skin, a god shows stars.

That starlight is the important part. Anything that comes from the gods carries a piece of the night sky with it. Their servants, the **Nyxborn**, are creatures born in that realm whose bodies open onto the constellations. Their gifts and their weapons carry the same sky. On Theros, an enchantment is very literally something the gods made.

## Why did Theros put stars on the card frame?

The first *Theros* set (September 2013) had a problem to solve and an idea to sell. The idea: the gods and everything they touch are enchantments. So the set was full of cards that were enchantments *and* something else:

- **Enchantment creatures** — the gods themselves, and the Nyxborn they send into the world.
- **Enchantment artifacts** — a cycle of five legendary weapons, one per god: Spear of Heliod, Bident of Thassa, Whip of Erebos, Hammer of Purphoros and Bow of Nylea.

The problem: on a crowded table, a creature that is also an enchantment looks like any other creature, and in this block the difference mattered constantly. The answer was a frame. Every enchantment creature and enchantment artifact in the block was printed with the starfield of Nyx showing through the upper part of its frame, so you could tell at a glance which permanents on the battlefield were also enchantments.

Two details from that first version are worth knowing:

- **The frame means nothing in the rules.** Wizards' mechanics article for the set says the Nyx frame "doesn't have any rules associated with it" — it is a reminder, not an ability.
- **Ordinary enchantments did not get it.** An Aura or a plain enchantment in the Theros block was printed on the regular frame. The stars marked the overlap, not the card type.

Enchantment creatures were not new, either. *Future Sight* had printed one in 2007 (Lucent Liminid), years before Theros. What Theros added was scale, a story reason, and a look.

<Callout tone="tip" title="The mechanics the frame travelled with">
  Theros' gods are legendary enchantment creatures that are only creatures while your **devotion** to their colour is high enough. **Bestow** lets a Nyxborn creature be cast as an Aura instead, and it turns back into a creature when the thing it enchants leaves. **Constellation**, from *Journey into Nyx*, rewards you each time an enchantment enters. None of them needs the frame, but all of them are easier to play when you can see your enchantments at a glance.
</Callout>

## How did the frame change over time?

By Scryfall's tagging, the three sets of the original block carry the frame on 25, 28 and 34 cards. After that it took a decade to go from one plane's signature to a rule of the game.

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| **2013–14** — *Theros*, *Born of the Gods*, *Journey into Nyx* | The frame debuts on enchantment creatures and enchantment artifacts. These sets predate the M15 redesign, so the starfield is drawn into the 2003-era frame. |
| **2015–18** — Commander and Masters reprints | Theros cards come back on the M15-era frame, and the starfield is redrawn to fit it. |
| **2020** — *Theros Beyond Death* | Magic returns to Theros. Mark Rosewater described the set's frame as a slightly tweaked version of the Nyx frame, and said a more universal enchantment-creature frame had been discussed. |
| **2022** — *Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty* | The turning point: a set on a different plane with dozens of enchantment creatures, all on the starfield frame. It stops being a Theros-only look. |
| **2023–24** — *March of the Machine* through *Duskmourn: House of Horror* | Enchantment creatures and enchantment artifacts wear the frame wherever they appear. |
| **November 2024** — *Magic: The Gathering Foundations* | The frame becomes the look of **every** enchantment, including Auras and plain enchantments. |

The Foundations change is the one that matters most to a card designer. Before it, the stars answered "is this creature also an enchantment?" After it, they answer "is this an enchantment?" — the same job the silver frame does for artifacts. Rosewater has said the change was discussed for *Duskmourn*, the set just before, but came too late to make it.

A few products still print enchantments the old way, mostly reprint sets that reproduce older cards as they were. If you see a recent plain enchantment without stars, that is usually why.

## How does the Nyx frame differ from the regular frame?

Less than you might expect, which is why it works. Put a Nyx card beside a regular card of the same colour and compare them part by part:

- **The outer frame** — the coloured field around the art and behind the bars — is where the stars are. On a regular card that field is a flat texture in the card's colour. On a Nyx card it is a night sky in that colour: brighter points near the top, fading down the sides.
- **The name bar, type bar and text box** are the same. Nothing about where you read the card changes.
- **The pinline, the border and the power/toughness box** are the same.
- **The legendary crown** carries the sky too. A legendary Nyx card — every god is one — shows the starfield in the strip inside the crown.
- **The colour still reads first.** A green Nyx card is plainly green and a gold one plainly gold; the stars sit in the colour rather than over it.

So the frame tells you one extra thing and takes nothing away. That restraint is the reason it could spread from 87 cards in one block to every enchantment in the game without anyone having to relearn how to read a card.

The original 2013–14 version differs in the way the whole card of that time differs: it sits in the 2003-era frame, with that era's bars and text box. If you are matching a custom card to the original Theros block, pair it with cards from the [2003 frame era](/articles/mtg-card-frame-eras-explained); for anything meant to sit beside modern cards, the current version is the right one.

## When should a custom card use it?

Follow the printed rule and your card will read the way players expect:

- **A card meant to look current** — any enchantment, Aura or enchantment creature — belongs on the Nyx frame. That is what a pack opened today looks like.
- **A card meant to look like 2014–2023** uses it only on enchantment creatures and enchantment artifacts, and keeps plain enchantments on the regular frame.
- **A card that is not an enchantment** should not wear it. The stars make a promise about the type line.

It is also a design prompt. The frame has always meant *made by something greater*: a god's servant, a spirit, a curse given a body. If your creature is the embodiment of an idea rather than an animal that happens to exist, making it an enchantment creature — and letting the frame say so — is often the right call. [How to design custom enchantments and artifacts](/articles/how-to-design-custom-enchantments-and-artifacts) covers the gameplay side of that choice, and [MTG card types explained](/articles/mtg-card-types-explained) covers what the extra type does in play.

## The Nyx frame in PipGlyph

In the [card creator](/create), the Nyx frame is under **Variations**:

- It is offered on any card whose type line says Enchantment — plain enchantments, Auras, enchantment creatures and enchantment artifacts.
- Pick it on a creature or an artifact and the word **Enchantment** is added to the type line for you, since the frame only appears on enchantments.
- A new enchantment starts on it, as a newly printed one would. You can switch back to the standard frame at any time.
- A legendary card gets the starfield crown.
- Cards you made before the frame arrived keep the look they have. Nothing changes until you choose it.
- A card you import follows its printing: the Theros gods arrive on the Nyx frame, and an enchantment printed before Foundations arrives on the frame it was printed with.

The stars do the same work on a custom card that they do on a printed one. They tell the table what the card is before anyone reads a word.

## FAQ

### Does the Nyx frame change how a card works?

No. The starfield is a visual reminder that the card is an enchantment, nothing more. Wizards said so when the frame was introduced in Theros, and it has stayed that way: a card plays exactly the same on a plain frame.

### Which cards get the Nyx frame today?

Since Magic: The Gathering Foundations (November 2024), new enchantments of every kind are printed on it — Auras and plain enchantments as well as enchantment creatures and enchantment artifacts. Before Foundations, only enchantments that were also creatures or artifacts wore it. Some reprint products still follow the older rule.

### Is the Nyx frame only for cards set on Theros?

Not any more. It began as the look of Theros' gods and their creations, but Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty used it on a different plane in 2022, and from Foundations onward it is simply the enchantment frame.

### Can I use the Nyx frame on a custom card in PipGlyph?

Yes. Any card whose type line says Enchantment can wear it: pick Nyx under Variations in the creator. Choosing it on a creature or an artifact adds the word Enchantment to the type line, because the frame only ever appears on enchantments.

### What is the difference between the Theros Nyx frame and today's?

The idea is the same — a night sky in the outer frame — but the drawing changed. The 2013–14 Theros block was printed on the 2003-era card frame, so its starfield sits in that older frame. Reprints from 2015 and Theros Beyond Death (2020) redrew it on the current M15-era frame, which is the version every new enchantment uses.
