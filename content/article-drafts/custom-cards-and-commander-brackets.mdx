A custom card belongs in the bracket of the most powerful thing it does. Put your card to the same questions the Commander bracket system asks of printed cards: does it lock out lands, chain extra turns, win with one other card, tutor cheaply, or go into every deck of its colors? Each "yes" raises its bracket. Then say so on the card's page and in the decklist.

## What are Commander brackets?

Commander brackets are a shared vocabulary for how strong a deck is, so four people can agree on a game before anyone shuffles. Wizards of the Coast and the Commander Format Panel introduced them in February 2025 with five tiers, and they have been revised since. In short:

| Bracket | Name | What a deck there looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Exhibition | A theme or a story first; winning is secondary |
| 2 | Core | Roughly a preconstructed deck: focused, fair, slower |
| 3 | Upgraded | Tuned and synergistic, with a few very strong cards |
| 4 | Optimized | As strong as a casual deck gets; few limits |
| 5 | cEDH | Competitive Commander, built to win quickly |

The tiers are guidance, not rules text. Their value is that they give a table the same words. A custom card needs those words even more than a printed one, because nobody at the table has seen it before.

## What makes a custom card raise a deck's bracket?

The bracket descriptions watch a handful of effect families. They are worth learning as families, because your custom card will rarely match a printed card exactly.

- **Mass land denial.** Destroying, bouncing or locking down every player's lands, or stopping lands from untapping, makes games that nobody gets to play. The bracket system keeps it to the top tiers.
- **Extra turns.** One extra turn is a big spell. A card that takes extra turns repeatedly, or that loops with another card, is a top-tier card.
- **Two-card combos.** If your card and exactly one other card win the game or create an infinite loop, the deck is playing for an early, sudden win. That belongs high on the ladder, and the earlier in the game it can happen, the higher.
- **Tutors.** Searching your library for a specific card makes a 100-card singleton deck play like a smaller, more consistent one. Lower brackets expect few of them.
- **Game Changers.** A short list of individual printed cards whose power or efficiency warps games. Lower brackets play none, and the middle allows only a few. At launch, bracket 3 allowed three.

## How do you tell if your custom card is a Game Changer?

A custom card is a Game Changer when it would go into nearly every deck of its colors, at almost any power level. That is the shared trait of the printed list. Those cards do one of three things for very little mana:

1. **They make outsized resources.** Mana well beyond a land drop, or cards well beyond one draw.
2. **They find what you need.** A specific card, on demand.
3. **They shut the table out.** They stop opponents from casting spells or drawing cards, or from untapping.

Test your design honestly. Write the card in the [card creator](/create), then picture it in three different decks of its colors: a tribal deck, a spellslinger deck and a lands deck. If it is the first card you would add to all three, it is a Game Changer whatever its rarity symbol says.

The common miss is the custom commander. A legendary creature that draws a card whenever anything happens, or that makes extra mana every turn, is a Game Changer that sits in the command zone, available in every game. If your commander does that, the deck is bracket 3 or higher before you add a single card.

## How do you cost a custom card for a target bracket?

Decide the bracket first, then design down to it. A card for bracket 2 should feel like a strong uncommon in a preconstructed deck. It asks for setup, it can be answered, and it does one thing well. A card for bracket 4 can be efficient and narrow, because the decks around it are too.

Three practical levers keep a card in its bracket:

- **Raise the floor cost before you cut the effect.** An extra generic mana often moves a card down a whole bracket without changing what it does.
- **Make the payoff conditional.** "Whenever you cast your second spell each turn" is a different card from "whenever you cast a spell".
- **Cap repetition.** "Once each turn" turns an engine into a bonus.

The guides on [designing balanced custom cards](/articles/designing-balanced-custom-mtg-cards) and [designing custom Commander cards](/articles/designing-custom-commander-cards) go deeper on costing against printed cards and multiplayer games.

## How should you label a custom card for your table?

Say the bracket out loud and in writing. Put it where people will see it before the game:

- **On the card page.** Add a tag such as "bracket 3" when you publish the card. Public and unlisted cards have a page your playgroup can open from a link.
- **On the deck.** Build the deck in [PipGlyph decks](/decks) and write the bracket and the one-line plan in its description. The deck page shows every custom card beside the real ones.
- **At the table.** Before the first shuffle, name the custom cards that pushed the bracket up. A fair warning costs nothing and earns trust.

If you are not sure, round up. A deck that plays below its stated bracket surprises nobody. A deck that plays above it ends the night early.

## A worked example

Here is one invented legendary creature, written two ways.

**Version A:** "Whenever you cast your first spell each turn, search your library for a card with mana value equal to that spell's mana value, reveal it, and put it into your hand." It tutors every turn, in every deck. Call it a Game Changer; the deck is bracket 3 at the least, and probably 4.

**Version B:** "Whenever you cast your first spell each turn, look at the top three cards of your library. Put one of them into your hand and the rest on the bottom in any order." It still rewards spells, but it digs instead of searching. It fits a bracket 2 deck and leaves room to build around it.

The theme is the same and the decks are different. Making that choice on purpose is what brackets are for. When you have one version written, [playtest it](/articles/how-to-playtest-custom-mtg-cards) against the decks your group actually plays.
